## Stories most relevant to PPPD / chronic dizziness

### Sara — "Chronic Dizziness and Imbalance Respond to Physiotherapy"
[`clean/chronic-dizziness-and-imbalance-respond-to-physiotherapy.md`](clean/chronic-dizziness-and-imbalance-respond-to-physiotherapy.md)

- A 48-hour acute episode of vomiting and vertigo; first diagnosed as
  "dehydration" at a medical centre.
- Then multiple doctors, multiple diagnoses (vestibular migraine,
  displaced crystals), Epley manoeuvres, medications — all failed.
- Left with serious balance issues, chronic dizziness, heavy head; had to
  resign from her job due to stress and inability to commute.
- Recovery took almost a year: found head-nodding/shaking rebalancing
  exercises online, then physiotherapy after an acute viral labyrinthitis
  diagnosis.
- Key message: physiotherapy works, it takes time, and you do get your
  life back. She suffered anxiety, fear of falling and isolation along
  the way.

### Shirley — "Face Your Fears… and Risk Getting Better!"
[`clean/face-your-fears-and-risk-getting-better.md`](clean/face-your-fears-and-risk-getting-better.md)

- Ear surgery in 1992 restored hearing but left her with vertigo; gave up
  running and dancing.
- Non-swimmer with a fear of water; water exercise was repeatedly
  suggested but even walking near water made her dizzy.
- After a car accident, physiotherapy in a private waist-deep pool, fully
  upright, clutching the rail — 14 sessions later she walked backwards in
  water while talking, to her own surprise.
- Outcome: vertigo around water lessened considerably. Fear-facing,
  gradual exposure worked.

### Karen — "How I Cope with Chronic Dizziness"
[`clean/how-i-cope-with-chronic-dizziness-karens-story.md`](clean/how-i-cope-with-chronic-dizziness-karens-story.md)

- Vertigo spells since 1999, worsening over the last 5 years; ~a dozen
  specialists before a neuro-otologist team at St. Paul's Hospital
  diagnosed Ménière's disease + migraine-associated vertigo.
- Symptoms: spinning, tinnitus, hearing loss, nausea, nystagmus, light and
  noise sensitivity, headaches, brain fog, and "drugged" eyes/head.
- Her coping framework — **the four Ps: Positive, Proactive, Patient,
  Perspective.**
  - **Positive:** control your mindset daily; list what you love and who
    supports you.
  - **Proactive:** research the condition, connect with fellow sufferers,
    track symptoms/triggers (food, activity, temperature, barometric
    pressure), educate family and friends.
  - **Patient:** bad days pass; pushing through frustration doesn't help.
  - **Perspective:** gratitude lists; remember others suffer worse.
- She accepts the "new normal", works minimally, volunteers, weight-trains
  when able, walks with husband and dogs, shops online.

### Nancy — "Vertigo Changed This Woman's Life for the Better"
[`clean/vertigo-changed-this-womans-life-for-the-better.md`](clean/vertigo-changed-this-womans-life-for-the-better.md)

- 28-year-old, diagnosed with vertigo by her physiotherapist after
  12-hour computer workdays and anxiety.
- Three weeks of forced rest, then continued computer sensitivity.
- Reframed vertigo as permission to rest: rediscovered paint-by-numbers
  art therapy, walks, guilt-free downtime.
- Outcome: still has vertigo but a new appreciation for life; her
  coaching business grew once she started listening to her body.

### Diane — "No Diagnosis: All Too Common"
[`clean/no-diagnosis-is-all-too-common-for-balance-and-dizziness-sufferers.md`](clean/no-diagnosis-is-all-too-common-for-balance-and-dizziness-sufferers.md)

- First episode ~15 years ago: woke up and fell back into bed, crawled to
  the bathroom. Intermittent unsteadiness since.
- Constant "zingy head" / ear buzz presumed tinnitus; never diagnosed.
- The site's editors note this is common and give a two-step plan:
  **educate yourself** (document symptoms precisely), then **advocate for
  yourself** (use your documentation to get referrals/second opinions).
  The editors say these steps lead to a proper diagnosis and treatment in
  ~95% of cases.

### Andrew — understanding employer made the difference

### Andrea — MRI accommodations for dizzy patients
[`clean/going-for-a-medical-procedure-ask-for-what-you-need-to-make-you-comfortable.md`](clean/going-for-a-medical-procedure-ask-for-what-you-need-to-make-you-comfortable.md)

- Dizzy spells from a past MRI left her dreading new scans; a spell costs
  her three "black hole" days.
- Her toolkit for both MRIs: bolster under knees, pad under lower back,
  two pillows (head not flat), earplugs + ear protectors for tinnitus.
- Both scans done with no dizzy spell. Lesson: **ask for what you need** —
  clinic staff can usually accommodate.

### Jay — CANVAS syndrome
[`clean/living-with-canvas-syndrome-jays-story.md`](clean/living-with-canvas-syndrome-jays-story.md)

- First symptom at ~30: a dry cough still present 40 years later. Balance

### Brandy — Mal de Débarquement Syndrome (MdDS)
[`clean/living-with-mal-de-debarquement-syndrome-mdds.md`](clean/living-with-mal-de-debarquement-syndrome-mdds.md)

- 24/7 rocking/bobbing since Christmas Eve 2012 (possibly triggered by a
  car-racing video game filling her visual field). Doctors chalked it up
  to anxiety; diagnosis took over a year (Mount Sinai, NYC).
- Only driving gives relief. Vacuuming, cooking, dishes make it worse.
  Looks fine — has a disabled parking permit and gets judgmental looks.
- Adaptations: holding onto things, nudging objects with shoulder/hip to
  signal the brain, locking eyes on a target. A five-month remission was
  "magical" — walking for hours, watching action movies, standing still.
  The MdDS Foundation Facebook group is her lifeline.

### David — Episodic Ataxia Type 2 (EA-2)
[`clean/living-life-with-episodic-ataxia-type-2-ea-2.md`](clean/living-life-with-episodic-ataxia-type-2-ea-2.md)

- Childhood: rubbery legs, dizziness, falling, funfair and train rides
  causing vomiting; hospital observations blamed "nerves."
- Adult: episodes misread as TIAs. Self-identified EA-2 online, got a
  referral to the one BC neurologist who knew it; genetic testing
  confirmed it.
- Acetazolamide helped the episodic attacks; balance still requires
  watching every footstep — uneven pavement can take him down.

### Muriel — "The Confusing Concussion"
[`clean/the-confusing-concussion.md`](clean/the-confusing-concussion.md)

## Living with an invisible illness — advice for family and friends

[`clean/but-you-dont-look-sick.md`](clean/but-you-dont-look-sick.md)

- On good days people with vestibular disorders look fine; at their worst
  they're house-bound, curled up, nauseated, holding walls. If vertigo
  strikes in public they're more likely seen as drunk than ill.
- Constant effort just to stay upright is exhausting. "Most of us would
  sacrifice a limb to make our heads work properly."
- Things family/friends can do:
  - Advocate with doctors, insurers and employers.
  - Research therapies.
  - Watch for new tech (PoNS, BalanceWear).
  - Find simple aids: cane, book stand, single-vision lenses.
  - Partner practically: window seats, lower shelves, an arm on stairs,
    smooth driving.
  - Ask how to help today.
  - Work with limitations (mornings are hardest).
  - Be part of the solution: grocery shopping, cleaning, cooking.
- Reference: Christine Miserandino's *Spoon Theory* for explaining hidden
  disability.

## Recurring themes across all stories

1. **Long diagnosis delays are the norm:** years and a dozen-plus
   specialists before answers; symptoms dismissed as anxiety or nerves.
2. **Self-advocacy is essential.** Research, document symptoms, demand
   referrals.
3. **Acceptance of a "new normal"** is a turning point, not defeat.
4. **Vestibular rehab/physiotherapy** is the single most-cited recovery
   tool.
5. **Small practical adaptations** (earplugs, pillows for MRIs, prong
   collars, window seats, holding onto things) restore daily function.
6. **Community matters.** Support groups, Facebook groups and volunteering
   show up again and again as lifelines.
7. **Hope is justified.** Remissions happen, trial devices help, progress
   is gradual, and recovery takes patience and effort.
